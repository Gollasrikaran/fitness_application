# NICE HTG238: Interspinous distraction procedures for lumbar spinal stenosis causing neurogenic claudication

Note: this NICE guidance specifically concerns a surgical/device intervention for lumbar spinal stenosis (interspinous distraction/spacer devices), rather than general lifestyle or exercise advice. NICE's general low back pain and sciatica guideline (NG59) also applies to people whose back/leg pain is caused by spinal stenosis; see the separate NG59-derived files for self-management and exercise guidance.

## About the condition

In lumbar spinal stenosis, the spinal canal (the tunnel through which nerves pass) narrows due to wear and tear of the spinal discs and joints. This narrowing can compress the nerves and cause neurogenic claudication — pain, numbness, weakness or tingling in the legs and buttocks that typically worsens with walking or standing and eases with sitting or bending forward.

## The procedure

Interspinous distraction (also called interspinous process decompression) is a minimally invasive alternative to laminectomy and spinal fusion, in which a small spacer implant is inserted between the spinous processes (the bony projections at the back of adjacent vertebrae) through a small incision, to hold them apart and relieve pressure on the compressed nerves.

## Guidance / Recommendation

Current evidence on interspinous distraction procedures for lumbar spinal stenosis causing neurogenic claudication shows that these procedures are efficacious for carefully selected patients in the short and medium term, although failure may occur and further surgery may be needed. There are no major safety concerns. Therefore, these procedures may be used provided that normal arrangements are in place for clinical governance, consent and audit.

Patient selection should be carried out by specialist spinal surgeons who are able to offer patients a range of surgical treatment options.
