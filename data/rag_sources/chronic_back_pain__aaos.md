# Low Back Pain

Almost every adult will experience low back pain at some point in their lives. Pain severity ranges from mild to severe, and may be temporary or chronic, sometimes accompanied by leg discomfort.

## Key Causes

**Overactivity and muscle strain**
Repetitive or unfamiliar activities cause muscle and ligament overstretching. Examples include seasonal sports or excessive yard work.

**Disk problems**
- Disk tears: small tears in the outer part of the disk occur naturally with aging; some cause no symptoms while others create lasting pain
- Disk herniation: the disk's inner material protrudes and puts pressure on the sensitive spinal nerves, causing pain
- Disk degeneration: age-related wearing causes disks to shrink and collapse

**Structural issues**
- Spondylolisthesis (vertebral slippage)
- Spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal)
- Scoliosis (abnormal spine curvature)
- Compression fractures from osteoporosis

## Symptoms

Pain characteristics vary: sharp, stabbing, dull, or cramping sensations. Patients often report that reclining or lying down improves their low back pain. Pain typically worsens with bending, lifting, sitting, and standing. Additional symptoms include sciatica (buttock and leg pain extending to the foot).

**Red flag symptoms requiring immediate medical attention:**
- Leg weakness
- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Fever, chills, or unexplained weight loss

## Diagnostic Tests

- Physical exam: range-of-motion assessment and nerve function testing
- Imaging: X-rays, MRI, CT scans, bone scans, and bone density tests

## Treatment Options

**Nonsurgical approaches**
- Medications: acetaminophen, NSAIDs, steroids, muscle relaxers
- Physical therapy with stretching and strengthening exercises
- Braces, chiropractic care, yoga, or Pilates

**Surgical options** (considered only after 6-12 months of conservative treatment)
- Spinal fusion
- Disk replacement
- Diskectomy (removing herniated disk material)
- Laminectomy (removing bone spurs)
- Kyphoplasty/vertebroplasty (cement injection for fractures)

## Prevention Strategies

- Regular aerobic exercise combined with back-strengthening routines
- Proper lifting technique: lift heavy items with your legs, not your back
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Avoiding smoking
- Using correct posture during standing, sitting, and lifting
