# Paget's disease of bone

Paget's disease of bone disrupts the normal cycle of bone renewal, causing bones to become weakened and possibly deformed. It's fairly common in the UK, particularly in older people, and is rare in people under 50. There's currently no cure, but treatment can help relieve symptoms.

Paget's disease of bone can affect 1 bone or several bones. The most commonly affected areas are the pelvis, spine and skull.

## Symptoms

Many people with Paget's disease of bone have no symptoms, and it's only found during tests carried out for another reason. When symptoms do occur, they can include:
- bone or joint pain
- a warm sensation over the affected bone
- changes in the shape of a bone
- a shooting pain that travels along or across the body, numbness and tingling (peripheral neuropathy), or balance problems, if a bone is pressing on a nerve

## Causes

Bone is constantly being renewed - old bone is broken down by cells called osteoclasts, and replaced with new bone by cells called osteoblasts. In Paget's disease of bone, the osteoclasts break down bone too quickly, and the new bone that's produced is larger but weaker than normal bone.

It's not clear what triggers this, but you're at a higher risk if you have a family history of Paget's disease of bone - you may inherit a genetic fault that means you're much more likely to develop the condition.

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you have persistent bone or joint pain, notice a change in the shape of a bone, or have numbness, tingling or balance problems that could suggest a bone is pressing on a nerve.

## Treatment

- **Bisphosphonate medicines** - help control bone regeneration and are used to control pain
- **Supportive therapies** - including physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and devices such as walking sticks or shoe inserts
- **Surgery** - may be needed if further problems develop, such as fractures, deformities or severe joint damage
- Getting enough calcium and vitamin D is also recommended

## Complications

Possible complications of Paget's disease of bone include fragile bones that are more likely to fracture, osteoarthritis, bone deformities, hearing loss (if the skull is affected), heart problems, and, rarely, bone cancer.
