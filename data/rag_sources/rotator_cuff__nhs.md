# Shoulder impingement (rotator cuff related shoulder pain)

Note: NHS.uk does not have a page titled specifically "rotator cuff" - rotator cuff tendon problems are covered on the NHS site under shoulder impingement syndrome, since impingement of a rotator cuff tendon is the underlying mechanism. Shoulder impingement is where a tendon inside your shoulder swells and rubs against tissue or bone, causing pain as you lift your arm. It typically improves within weeks or months and can be caused by irritation, injury, or degenerative wear of the tendon.

## Symptoms

- pain at the top and outer side of the shoulder, which may be worse at night
- shoulder pain that's worse when you lift your arm, especially when you lift it above your head
- weakness in the arm

## Self-care

**Do:**
- follow any exercises given to you by a GP or physiotherapist promptly
- apply an ice pack (wrapped in a towel) for up to 20 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day
- use over-the-counter painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, or ibuprofen gel

**Don't:**
- do not continue activities that bring on the pain, particularly repeated overhead lifting such as swimming or tennis
- do not completely stop moving the shoulder, as this can hinder recovery

## When to see a GP

See a GP if the shoulder pain persists for several weeks or is interfering with your daily activities. A physiotherapist can also diagnose this condition.

## Treatment

- prescription-strength painkillers
- physiotherapy with targeted exercises
- steroid injections if self-care and other conservative measures haven't been enough
- surgery, to widen the space around the tendon, in cases that don't respond to other treatment
