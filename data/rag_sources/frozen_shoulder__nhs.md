# Frozen shoulder

Frozen shoulder means your shoulder is painful and stiff for months, sometimes years. It can be treated with shoulder exercises and painkillers. The pain and stiffness will usually go away eventually, though recovery time varies significantly between people.

## Symptoms

- shoulder pain, which can be worse at night
- shoulder stiffness that limits how much you can move your arm

## Causes

Often there's no clear explanation, but frozen shoulder involves inflammation and tightening of the tissue around the shoulder joint. Risk factors include a previous injury or surgery that has limited arm movement, and diabetes.

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you have shoulder pain and stiffness that's not improving with self-care, or pain that's so bad it's stopping you moving your arm.

## Self-care

**Do:**
- follow any exercises given to you by a GP or physiotherapist
- keep moving your shoulder as much as you can
- take paracetamol regularly at the recommended dose
- put a heat pack (or a hot water bottle wrapped in a towel) on your shoulder for up to 20 minutes at a time

**Don't:**
- do not make up your own strenuous exercises or use gym equipment without advice

## Treatment

The NHS describes a step-by-step approach:
1. **Pain relief** - gentle movement, starting with paracetamol, then considering an oral NSAID such as ibuprofen if needed
2. **Stronger options** - prescription-strength painkillers or a steroid injection for short-term relief
3. **Restoring movement** - gentle exercises to do at home, with a possible referral to physiotherapy if pain persists

Physiotherapy usually lasts at least 6 weeks and can include stretching exercises, strengthening work, posture advice, and steroid injections. Many physiotherapists work directly within GP surgeries.
