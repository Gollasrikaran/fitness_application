# Neck pain and stiff neck

Most neck pain only lasts a few weeks and can often be managed with self-care, but it's worth seeing a GP if it doesn't improve.

## Causes

- sleeping in an awkward position
- poor posture, for example spending long periods sitting at a desk
- a pinched nerve
- an injury, such as whiplash

## Self-care

**Do:**
- take paracetamol or ibuprofen, or use ibuprofen gel
- use a low, firm pillow to support your neck while sleeping
- put a heat or cold pack on your neck
- try gentle neck-flexibility exercises

**Heat/cold application:**
- a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a towel, applied for about 5 minutes, up to 3 times a day
- a hot water bottle wrapped in a towel, applied for up to 20 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day

**Don't:**
- do not wear a neck collar or brace - try to keep your neck moving unless advised otherwise
- do not do activities that need a lot of neck movement, such as driving or cycling, if your neck movement is limited

## When to see a GP

See a GP if:
- the pain doesn't improve after a few weeks
- painkillers aren't helping
- you're worried about the pain
- you also have other symptoms, such as pins and needles or a cold arm

## Prevention

**Do:**
- try to keep your head level with your body when sleeping
- use a firm mattress
- sit upright with your shoulders back and relaxed

**Don't:**
- do not stay in the same position for long periods
- do not sleep on your front
- do not twist your neck awkwardly in bed

NHS community musculoskeletal (MSK) services may offer physiotherapy in many areas without needing a referral from a GP.
