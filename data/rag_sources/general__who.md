# Physical activity (WHO Fact Sheet)

This fact sheet summarizes the key content of the WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour (2020), published at https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240015128 (ISBN 9789240015128, released 25 November 2020). The full guideline document provides evidence-based public health recommendations for children, adolescents, adults, older adults, pregnant and postpartum women, and people living with chronic conditions or disability on the amount of physical activity (frequency, intensity and duration) required to offer significant health benefits and mitigate health risks. For the first time, the guideline also addresses associations between sedentary behaviour and health outcomes.

## Key Facts

Regular physical activity provides significant physical and mental health benefits. Globally, 31% of adults and 80% of adolescents do not currently meet the recommended levels of physical activity. The global cost of physical inactivity to health systems is estimated at approximately USD 27 billion annually through 2030 if trends do not improve.

## Definition and Scope

WHO defines physical activity as "any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that requires energy expenditure." This includes activity undertaken while working, playing, carrying out household chores, travelling, and engaging in recreational pursuits, at any level of skill.

## Health Benefits

**Children and adolescents:** Physical activity promotes bone health, healthy growth, and improved development of muscle and cardiorespiratory fitness, and improves cognitive outcomes (such as academic performance) and mental health (reduced symptoms of depression).

**Adults and older adults:** Regular physical activity is associated with reduced risk of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease mortality, incident hypertension, site-specific cancers, type 2 diabetes, and improved mental health, cognitive function, and sleep. For older adults, activity also helps prevent falls and bone-density related injuries and supports healthy bone and functional health.

**Pregnant and postpartum women:** Physical activity provides significant benefits with minimal risk, decreasing the risk of pre-eclampsia, gestational hypertension, gestational diabetes, excessive weight gain during pregnancy, and delivery complications, and helps decrease postpartum depression, with no evidence of harm to fetal outcomes.

**People living with chronic conditions or disability:** The guidelines note that adults and older adults living with chronic conditions (including hypertension, type 2 diabetes and HIV) or disability should follow the same overall physical activity patterns as the general population, with activity providing similar or greater health benefits than for the general population, and should consult a health professional to determine the type and amount of activity appropriate for their particular condition.

## Activity Recommendations (summary)

- Any amount of physical activity is better than none. If people are currently doing no physical activity, starting with small amounts and gradually increasing frequency, intensity and duration is a good way to begin.
- Adults should do at least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity, or at least 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity, or an equivalent combination, throughout the week.
- Adults should also do muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week, as these provide additional health benefits.
- Older adults should, as part of their weekly physical activity, do varied multicomponent physical activity that emphasizes functional balance and strength training at moderate or greater intensity on 3 or more days a week, to enhance functional capacity and prevent falls.
- All adults, including older adults, should limit the amount of time spent being sedentary. Replacing sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity (including light intensity) provides health benefits, and to help reduce the detrimental effects of high levels of sedentary behaviour on health, all adults and older adults should aim to do more than the recommended levels of moderate-to-vigorous intensity physical activity.
- For children and adolescents (5–17 years), an average of 60 minutes per day of moderate-to-vigorous intensity, mostly aerobic, physical activity across the week provides health benefits, and vigorous-intensity aerobic activities and those that strengthen muscle and bone should be incorporated at least 3 days a week.

## Global Trends

Approximately 1.8 billion adults worldwide are currently at risk of disease from not doing enough physical activity. Levels of insufficient physical activity are roughly twice as high in high-income countries (26%) compared with low-income countries (16%), and women are generally less active than men, with an average gap of 5 percentage points globally. If current trends continue, the global target of a 15% relative reduction in insufficient physical activity by 2030 will not be met.

## Policy / Member State Response

WHO's Global Action Plan on Physical Activity 2018–2030 calls on countries and communities to increase levels of physical activity in all age groups through measures that: create a more active society by changing social norms; create active environments with safe spaces and infrastructure; encourage active people through community-based programmes across multiple settings; and establish active systems through governance, leadership, multisectoral partnerships and workforce capacity building.
