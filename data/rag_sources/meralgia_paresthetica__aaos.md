# Burning Thigh Pain (Meralgia Paresthetica)

## Overview
A painful burning sensation on the outer thigh may indicate compression of the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (LFCN), a condition called meralgia paresthetica. The nerves in your body transmit sensory information to the brain and motor messages from the brain to muscles. When swelling, trauma, or pressure narrows the openings through which nerves pass, nerve compression can occur.

## Symptoms
Symptoms typically appear on one side of the body and include:
- Pain on the outer thigh, sometimes extending to the outer knee
- A burning sensation, tingling, or numbness in the same area
- Possible aching in the groin or pain across the buttocks
- Greater sensitivity to light touch than firm pressure in affected areas

## Doctor Examination

### Physical Examination
- Your doctor will check for sensory differences between the affected and unaffected leg
- The doctor will apply pressure on the nerve to reproduce the burning sensation
- An abdominal and/or pelvic examination may be necessary

### Tests
- X-rays identify potential bone abnormalities
- MRI or CT scans may be ordered if a growth is suspected
- Nerve conduction studies are rarely recommended
- Your doctor may inject numbing medication to confirm diagnosis

## Treatment
Treatment focuses on removing compression sources, which may involve:
- Resting from symptom-triggering activities
- Losing weight
- Wearing loose clothing
- Using alternative equipment instead of tool belts

In severe cases, corticosteroid injection to reduce inflammation may provide relief, with surgery rarely needed to release the nerve.
