# Trigger finger

Trigger finger causes a finger or thumb to become stuck in a bent position, with clicking or popping when the finger or thumb straightens.

## Symptoms

- a finger or thumb clicking or popping as it straightens
- pain in the finger or thumb
- stiffness
- difficulty straightening the affected digit without help

Symptoms are often worse in the morning and during activities that involve a lot of use of the hand.

## Causes

The exact cause isn't always known, though injury can trigger the condition. It happens when a tendon in the finger or thumb becomes thickened, which stops it moving smoothly and can cause it to become trapped, making the finger or thumb click or lock in a bent position.

Risk factors include:
- being over 40
- having diabetes
- having rheumatoid arthritis

## Self-care

- avoid or cut down on activities that make your symptoms worse
- take painkillers, such as ibuprofen, to help with pain

## When to see a GP

See a GP if your symptoms are not improving, or are affecting your daily activities.

## Treatment

- non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)
- a splint to keep the finger or thumb still
- a steroid injection to reduce swelling and pain
- surgery, if other treatments don't help

Many areas have NHS community musculoskeletal services that offer physiotherapy without needing a referral from a GP.
