# Plantar fasciitis

Plantar fasciitis causes pain on the bottom of the foot, around the heel and arch. Most people can manage the symptoms themselves, though it's worth seeing a GP if it hasn't improved after 2 weeks of self-care.

## Symptoms

Pain on the bottom of the foot, around the heel and arch, that:
- is often worse when you first stand up after resting or sleeping
- gets better with a bit of movement but worsens again after long periods of standing, walking, or running
- feels worse when you stretch your foot, such as pointing/flexing your toes or going up stairs

## Risk factors

- being aged 40 to 60
- doing exercises that put a lot of pressure on the feet, especially on hard surfaces
- being overweight, which puts extra pressure on your feet
- wearing unsupportive footwear
- activities that involve being on your feet a lot

## Self-care

**Do:**
- rest and raise your foot on a stool when you can
- put an ice pack on the painful area for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
- wear shoes with cushioned heels and good arch support
- use insoles or heel pads in your shoes
- try gentle stretching exercises
- try wearing shoes that fit properly
- try low-impact exercise such as swimming
- take painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen
- try to lose weight if you're overweight

**Don't:**
- do not stand for long periods of time
- do not wear high heels or tight, pointy shoes
- do not wear flip-flops or backless slippers
- do not walk barefoot on hard surfaces

## When to see a GP

- the pain is severe or stopping you doing normal activities
- the pain is getting worse or keeps coming back
- the pain has not improved after treating it at home for 2 weeks
- you have tingling or loss of feeling in your foot
- you have diabetes - foot problems can be more serious if you have diabetes

## Treatment

A physiotherapist can provide targeted exercises, and a podiatrist can advise on insoles or orthotics and suitable footwear. Steroid injections and extracorporeal shock-wave therapy may be used for persistent cases, and surgery is considered only rarely.
