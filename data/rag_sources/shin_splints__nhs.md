# Shin splints

Shin splints is a type of shin pain, usually caused by exercise. It's not serious and there are things you can do to help get better. Shin splints usually get better within a few weeks.

## Symptoms

Pain and tenderness along the front of your lower leg (shin), usually brought on by exercise such as running.

## Self-care

**Do:**
- use painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen gel (or ibuprofen tablets if needed) to ease the pain
- put an ice pack (or a bag of frozen vegetables) wrapped in a towel on your shin for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
- switch to gentle exercise such as yoga or swimming while healing
- exercise on soft ground once you're feeling better
- warm up before exercise and stretch after exercise
- make sure your trainers or shoes support your feet properly

**Don't:**
- do not continue doing the exercise that caused your shin splints
- do not rush back into exercise at the level you were at before - build your exercise routine back up slowly

## Risk factors

Shin splints are more likely after a period of inactivity, when running on hard surfaces, or if you're using an incorrect running technique.

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you've tried self-care for shin splints but the pain is getting worse or is not getting better. Call NHS 111 or get help from 111 online if the pain is severe, or if it followed an injury.

## Treatment

A GP can usually diagnose shin splints by examining your leg and asking about your symptoms. If your symptoms are not improving, you may be referred for physiotherapy, which is available free of charge on the NHS, although waiting times can be long.
