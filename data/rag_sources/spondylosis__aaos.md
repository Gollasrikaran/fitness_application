# Cervical Spondylosis: Arthritis of the Neck

## Overview
Neck pain frequently stems from age-related changes. Cervical spondylosis, commonly called arthritis of the neck, is the medical term for these age-related, wear-and-tear changes that occur over time. This condition affects over 85% of people older than 60, though many experience no symptoms. Most cases respond well to conservative approaches combining medication and physical therapy.

## Anatomy
The cervical spine consists of seven vertebrae forming the neck. The spine contains the spinal cord and nerve roots, intervertebral disks (shock absorbers made of an outer annulus fibrosus and soft nucleus pulposus center), and facet joints providing stability.

## Causes
Cervical spondylosis results from normal degenerative aging. As disks lose water content and height, they weaken. Increased pressure causes facet joints to deteriorate. Cartilage wearing away can trigger bone spur formation, potentially narrowing the space for nerves (stenosis).

**Risk factors include:**
- Age (most significant factor)
- Family history of neck problems
- Smoking
- Repetitive occupational neck motion
- Depression or anxiety
- Previous neck trauma

## Symptoms
Many people remain asymptomatic. When present, symptoms include:
- Mild to severe neck pain and stiffness
- Pain worsening with prolonged neck positioning
- Headaches
- Grinding sensations during neck movement
- Numbness/weakness in arms and hands (if nerve compression occurs)
- Balance problems or muscle spasms

## Doctor Examination
Physicians conduct thorough physical examinations testing arm and hand strength, reflexes, sensation, flexibility, and gait. Diagnostic imaging includes X-rays (showing bone alignment), MRI scans (revealing soft tissue damage), CT scans (detailed bone visualization), myelograms (using contrast dye), and electrodiagnostic testing measuring muscle electrical activity.

## Treatment Options

**Nonsurgical approaches** (typically first-line):
- Physical therapy with strengthening/stretching exercises, lasting 6-8 weeks
- Medications: acetaminophen, NSAIDs, oral corticosteroids, muscle relaxants
- Soft cervical collar for short-term use
- Ice/heat therapies
- Steroid-based injections (epidural blocks, facet joint blocks, radiofrequency ablation)

**Surgical treatment** is reserved for cases involving pinched nerves or spinal cord compression with progressive neurological symptoms, or severe pain unresponsive to conservative treatment.
