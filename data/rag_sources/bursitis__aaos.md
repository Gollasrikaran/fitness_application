# Hip Bursitis: Comprehensive Overview

## What Are Bursae?
Bursae are small, jelly-like sacs that are located throughout the body, including around the shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, and heel. These structures contain fluid and function as cushions between bones and soft tissues, reducing friction during movement.

## Types of Hip Bursitis
Two major bursae in the hip can become inflamed:

1. **Trochanteric bursitis**: Affects the bursa covering the greater trochanter (bony hip point), causing lateral hip pain
2. **Iliopsoas bursitis**: Involves the bursa on the groin side of the hip, less common but treated similarly

## Symptoms
Patients typically experience sharp, intense pain at the hip point that may extend to the outer thigh. Early-stage symptoms intensify at night, when lying on the affected side, and when rising from seated positions. Pain may worsen with prolonged walking, stair climbing, or squatting.

## Risk Factors
Hip bursitis occurs more frequently in women and middle-aged or elderly populations. Contributing factors include:
- Repetitive stress from running, cycling, or prolonged standing
- Direct hip trauma or prolonged pressure on one side
- Spine conditions affecting gait
- Tight iliotibial (IT) band creating bursal pressure
- Leg-length discrepancies
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Prior hip surgery or prosthetic implants
- Bone spurs or calcium deposits

## Diagnosis
Doctors perform physical examinations checking for tenderness over the hip bone. Additional imaging such as X-rays, bone scans, and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans may rule out other conditions.

## Treatment Options

### Nonsurgical Approaches
Conservative treatment includes activity modification, NSAIDs like ibuprofen for pain and inflammation, assistive devices (canes or crutches), and physical therapy focusing on IT band stretching and hip strengthening. Injection of a corticosteroid may also help to relieve symptoms of hip bursitis through office-based procedures, though repeated injections risk tissue damage.

### Surgical Treatment
Surgery remains rare and typically follows failed conservative approaches. Options include traditional bursa removal or endoscopic removal through small incisions, both performed on an outpatient basis.

## Recovery and Prevention
Post-surgical recovery is brief, with most patients walking the evening after surgery. Prevention strategies emphasize avoiding repetitive hip stress, maintaining hip strength and flexibility, managing weight, and using proper shoe inserts for leg-length differences.
