# Slipped disc

A slipped disc, also called a prolapsed or herniated disc, is when a soft cushion of tissue between the bones (vertebrae) in your spine bulges outwards.

## Symptoms

Common signs include:
- Lower back pain
- Neck pain/discomfort
- Numbness or tingling in the arms, legs, hands or feet
- Muscle weakness
- Sciatic pain that travels from the lower back down the leg (sciatica)

Not all slipped discs cause symptoms.

## Self-management strategies

**Activity:** Gradual, gentle exercise such as walking, swimming and yoga helps recovery better than prolonged rest.

**Pain relief:** Anti-inflammatory medications like ibuprofen, taken regularly at recommended doses, are preferred. Stronger codeine-based painkillers exist for new/severe pain but carry addiction risks and should only be used short-term.

**Professional advice:** A pharmacist can recommend suitable pain management options.

## When to see a GP

Seek GP care if:
- Painkillers aren't helping
- Pain persists beyond a few weeks
- You develop a fever
- You have unexplained weight loss
- There's swelling in your back
- Pain worsens at night

## Emergency care (call 999)

Seek emergency help for:
- Numbness around your genital or anal area
- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Loss of feeling in one or both legs
- Symptoms following an accident or injury

## Medical treatment

Diagnosis typically relies on a description of symptoms and a physical examination. Treatments may include stronger painkillers, steroid injections, muscle relaxants, physiotherapy, or MRI imaging to confirm the diagnosis. Surgery is rare and reserved for cases with progressive weakness or numbness.

## Prevention

- Maintain regular physical activity
- Use proper lifting techniques
- Avoid smoking, to help protect spinal disc tissue
