# Herniated Disk in the Lower Back

## What Is It?

A herniated disk occurs when the soft, jelly-like center of the disk pushes through the tough outer ring. This condition most frequently affects the lower back and can result from age-related wear (disk degeneration) or a traumatic injury.

## Common Symptoms

Patients typically experience lower back pain initially, which may improve after several days. Additional symptoms include:
- Sharp, often shooting pain that travels from the buttock down the back of one leg (sciatica)
- Leg numbness or tingling
- Leg weakness
- Rarely, loss of bladder or bowel control, which indicates cauda equina syndrome — a medical emergency

## Risk Factors

Contributing risk factors include: being male, aged 20-50; improper lifting technique; excess body weight; repetitive activities that strain the spine; prolonged sitting or a sedentary lifestyle; and smoking.

## Treatment Approaches

**Nonsurgical options (typically attempted first):**
- Brief bed rest (1-2 days maximum)
- Anti-inflammatory medications
- Physical therapy
- Epidural steroid injections

**Surgical procedures (for persistent symptoms or neurological deficits):**
- Microdiskectomy — the most common surgical approach
- Endoscopic diskectomy — a newer, minimally invasive alternative

## Prognosis

Most people feel better within a few weeks or months without surgery, with typical recovery occurring within 3-4 months. However, there is up to a 20 to 25% chance of disk reherniation during one's lifetime.
