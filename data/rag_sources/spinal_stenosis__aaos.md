# Lumbar Spinal Stenosis

Lumbar spinal stenosis is a common cause of low back and leg pain, or sciatica. The condition develops as the spine ages and degenerative changes narrow the spinal canal, compressing the nerves. It most commonly affects adults over 60.

## Causes

Arthritis represents the primary cause. As spinal disks lose water content with age, they shrink and collapse. This settling creates two problems: increased pressure on the facet joints (triggering arthritis) and shrinking of the nerve exit tunnels. Degenerating facet joints develop bone spurs, and thickened ligaments further crowd the nerve space.

## Symptoms

Common presentations include:
- Back pain (variable intensity)
- Burning pain in the buttocks or legs (sciatica)
- Numbness or tingling sensations
- Leg weakness or foot drop
- Cauda equina syndrome (a medical emergency involving bowel/bladder dysfunction)

Patients typically feel better when leaning forward or sitting; standing and walking worsen the pain.

## Nonsurgical Treatment Options

- Physical therapy and core strengthening
- NSAIDs for inflammation reduction
- Epidural steroid injections (a maximum of three per year)
- Acupuncture and chiropractic manipulation (with caution)
- Lumbar traction (limited scientific evidence of benefit)

## Surgical Treatment

Two main approaches:

**Laminectomy:** Removes bone, spurs, and ligaments compressing the nerves. Can use open or minimally invasive techniques.

**Spinal fusion:** Combines decompression with stabilization for arthritis-related instability.

Recovery typically involves a hospital stay, physical therapy, and a gradual return to activities over weeks to months.
