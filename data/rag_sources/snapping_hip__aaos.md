# Snapping Hip

## Overview
Snapping hip is a condition where you experience a snapping sensation or hear a popping sound in your hip during activities like walking, standing from a chair, or swinging your leg. The snapping sensation occurs when a muscle or tendon moves over a bony protrusion in your hip. While typically painless and harmless, it can be bothersome and occasionally progresses to bursitis, which causes painful inflammation.

## Anatomy
The hip functions as a ball-and-socket joint where the femur's rounded end fits into a cup-shaped socket (acetabulum) in the pelvis. The acetabulum is surrounded by fibrocartilage called the labrum that provides stability. Ligaments surround and hold the joint together, with tendons attaching muscles from the buttocks, thighs, and pelvis to bones to control movement. Fluid-filled sacs called bursae are positioned strategically around the hip to provide cushioning and facilitate smooth muscle movement over bone.

## Description

### Outside of the Hip
The most common snapping hip location occurs where the iliotibial (IT) band passes over the greater trochanter of the femur. When the hip is straight, the iliotibial band is behind the trochanter. When the hip bends, the band moves over the trochanter so that it is in front of it. The IT band's constant tightness combined with the trochanter's slight protrusion creates the characteristic snapping sound. Repeated snapping may lead to bursitis, involving thickening and inflammation of the fluid-filled bursa.

### Front of the Hip
The rectus femoris tendon, running from the front thigh to the pelvis, can cause snapping at the hip's front. As you bend the hip, the tendon shifts across the head of the thighbone, and when you straighten the hip, the tendon moves back to the side. This back-and-forth motion produces the snapping sensation. Additionally, the iliopsoas tendon may catch on bony prominences at the front of the pelvis bone.

### Back of the Hip
Hamstring tendons attaching to the sitting bone (ischial tuberosity) can create snapping in the buttock region when they move across this bony point. This form occurs less frequently than other snapping hip types.

### Cartilage Problems
The labrum that lines the socket of the hip can tear and cause a snapping sensation. Damaged cartilage may loosen and float within the joint, causing catching or locking. Although not technically true snapping hip, torn labrum symptoms may resemble it, though typically producing deeper groin pain.

## Cause
Snapping hip most commonly results from tightness in surrounding muscles and tendons. People who are involved in sports and activities that require repeated bending at the hip are more likely to experience snapping hip. Dancers face particular vulnerability. Young athletes commonly experience snapping hip due to muscle tightness during adolescent growth spurts.

## Home Remedies
For non-painful snapping hip, conservative treatments include:
- Reducing activity level and applying ice to the affected area
- Using nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs like naproxen or ibuprofen for discomfort reduction
- Modifying sports or exercise activities to avoid repetitive hip movement, such as reducing cycling time or swimming using only arm movements

Consult a doctor if discomfort persists after these conservative approaches.

## Doctor Examination

### Medical History and Physical Examination
Physicians determine the snapping's exact cause through history and physical examination, which may include:
- Asking about pain location, activities causing snapping, ability to demonstrate it, and prior hip injuries
- Requesting hip movement in various directions to reproduce symptoms while feeling tendon movement during hip bending or extension

### Imaging Tests
X-rays provide clear pictures of dense structures, such as bone. Although snapping hip X-rays typically appear normal, doctors may order imaging to rule out bone or joint problems.

## Treatment
Initial treatment involves rest and activity modification. Depending on the snapping's cause, additional nonsurgical options may be recommended.

### Physical Therapy
Doctors may prescribe stretching and strengthening exercises with possible physical therapist guidance:

**Iliotibial band stretch:**
- Stand next to a wall for support
- Cross the leg closest to the wall behind the other leg
- Lean your hip toward the wall until feeling a stretch on the hip's outside; hold 30 seconds
- Repeat on the opposite side
- Perform 2-3 sets of 4 repetitions per side

**Piriformis stretch:**
- Lie on your back with bent knees and feet flat
- Cross the affected hip's foot over the opposite knee and clasp hands behind the thigh
- Pull the thigh toward you until feeling stretch in hip and buttocks; hold 30 seconds
- Repeat on opposite side
- Perform 2-3 sets of 4 repetitions per side

### Corticosteroid Injection
For hip bursitis cases, corticosteroid injection into the bursa may reduce painful inflammation.

### Surgical Treatment
When snapping hip resists nonsurgical treatment, surgery may be recommended based on the underlying cause.
- **Hip arthroscopy**: During hip arthroscopy, your surgeon inserts a small camera, called an arthroscope, into your hip joint. The camera displays images guiding miniature instruments through small incisions. Hip arthroscopy is most often used to remove or repair fragments of a torn labrum.
- **Open procedure**: Traditional larger surgical incisions may be necessary to better visualize and access the snapping hip's cause. Your orthopaedic surgeon will discuss the optimal procedure for your individual health needs.
