# Standard Treatment Workflow (STW): Neck Pain (ICD-10-M54.2)

Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India / Indian Council of Medical Research. Published July 2024.

Note: "KEEP A HIGH THRESHOLD FOR INVASIVE PROCEDURES"

## History to Take

- Acute or chronic
- Severity of pain
- Site and onset of pain
- Radiation of pain
- Associated features such as fever, trauma, weight lifting, associated tingling or numbness
- Anticonvulsants and steroids intake

## Red Flag Signs (consider referral to a higher center for evaluation)

- Features of neurological deficit including sudden onset of loss of bladder/bowel control, numbness/paresthesias/weakness of upper limbs or lower limbs
- Severe worsening pain, especially at night or when moving the neck
- Significant history of trauma
- Weight loss, fever, history of cancer
- Use of prolonged steroids or intravenous drugs
- First episode of severe pain in a patient over 50 years of age

## Assessment

Conduct a full assessment including:
- History taking
- Physical and neurological examination
- Evaluation of red flags
- Psychosocial risk factors

If red flag signs are present: refer to a higher centre for evaluation.

If no red flag signs: proceed to lab test and imaging based on provisional diagnosis, and manage according to duration (acute/sub-acute vs chronic).

**Lab and imaging tests (based on provisional diagnosis):**
- Lab tests: Hemogram with ESR and CRP
- Imaging: X-rays of the Cervical Spine - Anteroposterior (AP) and lateral views; MRI of the Cervical spine

## Acute and Sub-acute Neck Pain (duration less than 12 weeks)

- Consider analgesics for short duration: Acetaminophen/PCM and NSAIDs; short course muscle relaxants; use opioids (short duration) if not responding to the above analgesics
- Immobilize neck in acute stage. Once pain subsides, start neck strengthening exercises and/or physical activity
- Recommend neck strengthening exercises and/or physical activity (when pain becomes bearable)
- Avoid lifting heavy weights
- Prescribe self-care strategies: alternating cold and heat therapy; continuation of Activities of Daily Living as tolerated
- Encourage early return to work
- Educate the patient that neck pain usually resolves with time
- Re-assess at 2-6 weeks (including red flags) if the patient is not returning to normal function or symptoms are worsening
- If not improving, consider lab test and imaging, and consider referral to: a physical therapist; an orthopaedic surgeon (for unresolving radicular symptoms); or a multidisciplinary pain program (if not returning to work)

## Chronic Neck Pain (duration greater than 12 weeks)

- Consider lab test and imaging (X-rays)
- Prescribe neck strengthening exercises or therapeutic exercises
- Analgesic options: Acetaminophen/Paracetamol (PCM); NSAIDs; short-term muscle relaxant for flare-ups
- If pain is not responding to the above: opioids for short-term use in severe pain; low-dose antidepressants for short duration
- Other modalities: physiotherapy; TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) as an adjunct to active therapy, etc.
- If pain still does not subside, consider referral to a referral centre, where modalities include: a multidisciplinary chronic pain program/clinic; and surgery in carefully selected patients after expert opinion and based on indications

## Abbreviations

AP: Antero-Posterior. CRP: C-reactive Protein. ESR: Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate. MRI: Magnetic Resonance Imaging. NSAIDs: Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs. TENS: Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation.
