# Scoliosis

Scoliosis is where the spine twists and curves to the side. It can affect people of any age, from babies to adults, but most often starts in children aged 10 to 15. Mild scoliosis often does not need treatment, but more severe cases may need treatment to help stop the curve getting worse or to help improve posture.

## Symptoms

- a visibly curved spine
- leaning to one side
- uneven shoulders
- one shoulder or hip sticking out
- ribs sticking out further on one side
- clothes not fitting well
- back pain (this is more common in adults with scoliosis)

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you think you or your child might have scoliosis. They may refer you to a specialist and arrange an X-ray to check how severe the curve is.

**Scoliosis Support & Research** can help you find specialist centres.

## Causes

In around 80% of cases the cause is unknown - this is called idiopathic scoliosis. It's not known to be caused, or made worse, by things like poor posture, exercise or diet, but it does sometimes run in families.

Less common causes include:
- **Congenital scoliosis** - the bones in the spine did not form properly while the baby was in the womb
- **Neuromuscular scoliosis** - abnormal development of the nerves and muscles, associated with conditions such as cerebral palsy
- **Degenerative scoliosis** - wear and tear of the spine as you age

## Treatment

Treatment depends on age, how severe the curve is, and whether it's likely to get worse.

- **Babies and toddlers** - may not need treatment, or a brace may be used to help stop the curve getting worse as they grow
- **Older children** - a back brace may be worn while the spine is still growing; surgery is occasionally used to help control growth of the spine
- **Adults** - treatment focuses on pain relief with painkillers, spinal injections, or in rare cases surgery

Exercise is beneficial for general health, unless a medical professional advises otherwise.

## Living with scoliosis

Most people with scoliosis can live a normal, active life. The condition usually stabilises once growth has finished and usually causes minimal health problems, although some people find it helpful to get support for the psychological effects.
