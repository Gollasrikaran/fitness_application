## What is Osteoarthritis?

Osteoarthritis is the most common form of arthritis among older people and affects roughly one-third of adults aged 65+. The condition develops when cartilage — the cushioning tissue at bone ends — deteriorates, potentially allowing bones to contact each other directly. This primarily impacts hands, knees, hips, spine, neck, and lower back, though it affects just joints, not internal organs.

## What Causes Osteoarthritis?

The disease stems from years of wear and tear on joints affecting cartilage structure. Contributing factors include advancing age, repetitive joint stress from prior injuries, misaligned joints, and excess body weight. Research suggests osteoarthritis results from a combination of factors in the body and the environment.

## What Are the Symptoms?

Common warning indicators include joint pain, swelling or tenderness in one or more joints, stiffness following rest periods, and a crunching feeling or sound of bone rubbing on bone. Notably, only approximately one-third of individuals with X-ray evidence of osteoarthritis actually experience noticeable symptoms.

## What Are the Treatments?

Management strategies encompass exercise (strengthening, aerobic, range-of-motion activities), weight control, medications (acetaminophen or NSAIDs), heat/cold therapy, and surgical interventions when necessary. Options include arthroscopic debridement, bone repositioning, or joint replacement. Current treatments can relieve symptoms such as pain and disability, but right now there are no treatments that can cure osteoarthritis.
