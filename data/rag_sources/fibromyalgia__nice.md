# NICE Guideline NG193: Chronic Pain (Primary and Secondary) in Over 16s - Assessment of All Chronic Pain and Management of Chronic Primary Pain (Recommendations)

Published: 7 April 2021. Note: this guideline does not use the term "fibromyalgia" as a section heading; instead it defines fibromyalgia as a type of chronic primary pain and gives recommendations for managing chronic primary pain as a category. Per the guideline's own "Terms used in this guideline" section: "Fibromyalgia (chronic widespread pain) is a type of chronic primary pain." The extracts below focus on the definition of chronic primary pain and the management recommendations (section 1.2) most relevant to fibromyalgia/chronic widespread pain.

## 1.1 Assessing All Types of Chronic Pain

This guideline covers chronic pain that "persists or recurs for more than 3 months," including chronic primary pain (in which no underlying condition adequately accounts for the pain or its impact) and chronic secondary pain, which can coexist. "Think about a diagnosis of chronic primary pain if there is no clear underlying (secondary) cause or the pain or its impact is out of proportion to any observable injury or disease, particularly when the pain is causing significant distress and disability." Recognise that an initial diagnosis of chronic primary pain may change with time, and re-evaluate if the presentation changes.

Offer a person-centred assessment to identify factors contributing to the pain and how it affects the person's life, including lifestyle, day-to-day activities, work and sleep, physical and psychological wellbeing, stressful life events, and social interaction. Discuss with the person and their family or carers that symptoms may fluctuate and flare-up, that a reason for the pain may not be identified, that pain may not improve or may need ongoing management, and that quality of life can still improve even if pain remains unchanged.

## 1.2 Managing Chronic Primary Pain

### Exercise Programmes and Physical Activity

"Offer a supervised group exercise programme to people aged 16 years and over to manage chronic primary pain. Take people's specific needs, preferences and abilities into account." Encourage people with chronic primary pain "to remain physically active for longer-term general health benefits."

### Psychological Therapy

Consider acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) or cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) for pain, delivered by healthcare professionals with appropriate training. "Do not offer biofeedback to people aged 16 years and over to manage chronic primary pain."

### Acupuncture

Consider a single course of acupuncture or dry needling (within a traditional Chinese or Western system) only if it is delivered in a community setting, by a band 7 (or lower) healthcare professional with appropriate training, and made up of no more than 5 hours of healthcare professional time (or delivered equivalently for the same or lower cost).

### Electrical Physical Modalities

"Do not offer any of the following to people aged 16 years and over to manage chronic primary pain because there is no evidence of benefit: TENS; ultrasound; interferential therapy."

### Pharmacological Management

Consider an antidepressant (amitriptyline, citalopram, duloxetine, fluoxetine, paroxetine or sertraline) for people aged 18 and over, after a full discussion of benefits and harms, explaining that these medicines "may help with quality of life, pain, sleep and psychological distress, even in the absence of a diagnosis of depression." Seek specialist advice before considering antidepressants for young people aged 16-17.

"Do not initiate any of the following medicines to manage chronic primary pain in people aged 16 years and over": antiepileptic drugs including gabapentinoids (except in a clinical trial for complex regional pain syndrome); antipsychotic drugs; benzodiazepines; corticosteroid trigger point injections; ketamine; local anaesthetics; local anaesthetic/corticosteroid combination trigger point injections; NSAIDs; opioids; paracetamol. For people already taking these medicines, review prescribing as part of shared decision making — explaining the lack of evidence for chronic primary pain, and agreeing a shared plan to continue safely at a safe dose with few harms, or to reduce and stop if there is little benefit or significant harm.

## Terms Used in This Guideline

**Chronic pain**: Pain that persists or recurs for more than 3 months, including both chronic primary and chronic secondary pain, which can coexist.

**Chronic primary pain**: Has no clear underlying condition, or the pain/impact is out of proportion to any observable injury or disease. "The mechanisms underlying chronic primary pain are only partially understood." This guideline is consistent with the ICD-11 definition. "Fibromyalgia (chronic widespread pain) is a type of chronic primary pain." ICD-11 also categorises complex regional pain syndrome, chronic primary headache and orofacial pain, chronic primary visceral pain, and chronic primary musculoskeletal pain as types of chronic primary pain.

**Flare-up**: "A sudden, temporary worsening of symptoms. Usually this refers to more intense pain on a day-to-day basis. It can also refer to a change in fatigue, stiffness, function or disease activity."

**Pain management programme**: "Any intervention that has 2 or more components, including a physical and a psychological component, delivered by trained people, with some interaction/coordination between the 2 components."
