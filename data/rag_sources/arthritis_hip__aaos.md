# Hip Osteoarthritis: Clinical Overview

## Description
Osteoarthritis represents a degenerative joint condition affecting individuals primarily over age 50, though younger people may develop it. The disorder involves gradual deterioration of cartilage that normally cushions the hip joint. As this protective tissue wears away, bones may rub together, and damaged areas can develop bone spurs to compensate for lost cartilage.

## Causes & Risk Factors
The condition lacks a single definitive cause but occurs more frequently in individuals with:
- Advanced age
- Family history of the disease
- Prior hip joint injuries
- Excess body weight
- Congenital hip malformation (developmental dysplasia)

Development remains possible without these risk factors.

## Symptoms
Pain represents the primary symptom, typically developing gradually and intensifying over time. Patients commonly experience:
- Groin or thigh discomfort radiating toward buttocks or knee
- Morning stiffness or post-activity soreness
- Joint "locking" or grinding sensations
- Reduced hip mobility affecting walking gait
- Symptoms worsening with vigorous activity or rainy weather

## Doctor Examination
Physicians conduct physical assessments examining hip tenderness, joint mobility, gait patterns, and surrounding tissue integrity. Diagnostic imaging — particularly X-rays — reveals joint space narrowing, bone changes, and osteophyte formation.

## Treatment Options

**Nonsurgical approaches include:**
- Activity modification and weight management
- Physical therapy for strengthening and flexibility
- Assistive walking devices (canes, walkers)
- Medications: acetaminophen, NSAIDs, corticosteroid injections

**Surgical interventions involve:**
- Total hip replacement (most common)
- Hip resurfacing procedures
- Osteotomy (bone realignment)

Recovery timelines vary by procedure type; physical therapy typically supports rehabilitation.
