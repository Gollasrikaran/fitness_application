# Claw Toe

## Overview
Claw toe is a common foot deformity in which the affected toe curls downward. The curled toe digs into the sole of the shoe, which can create painful thickened areas of skin (calluses). If left untreated, the deformity tends to progress and may eventually become permanent (rigid).

## Anatomy
The foot has five toes, and each has three joints except the big toe, which has two. In claw toe, the affected toe bends downward at two locations: the proximal interphalangeal joint (the middle joint) and the distal interphalangeal joint (the joint closest to the toe tip).

## Cause
Several factors contribute to this deformity:

- **Footwear.** Tight shoes — those that are too short or have high heels — are frequently a cause.
- **Nerve damage.** Conditions such as diabetes, stroke, and alcohol use can weaken muscles by damaging nerve function.
- **Other causes.** Injuries and inflammatory conditions can also produce this foot abnormality.

## Symptoms
Signs of claw toe include:

- The toe extends upward from the joint where it meets the foot
- The toe bends downward at the middle joint, toward the sole
- The toe may also bend downward at the joint nearest the tip, curling under the foot
- Thickened skin (calluses or corns) may develop on top of the toe or under the ball of the foot

## Doctor Examination
A doctor should evaluate the condition and may order tests to rule out nerve conditions causing muscle imbalance. Determining whether the deformity is still flexible or has become fixed (rigid) affects treatment choices. The doctor will also check the skin and any calluses that have formed, which influences the treatment plan.

## Treatment

### Nonsurgical Treatment (Early Stage)
- Splinting or taping the toe to hold it in a better position
- Choosing shoes with a roomy toe box and avoiding tight shoes
- Manually stretching the toe toward a more normal position
- Strengthening exercises such as picking up marbles with the toes and towel curls

### Nonsurgical Treatment (Advanced Stage)
- Padding to redistribute pressure and reduce stress on the ball of the foot
- "Deep" or extra-depth shoes that provide additional room (about 3/8 inch of extra depth)
- Professional shoe modification to create extra room for the toe

## Surgical Treatment
**Flexible deformities.** Surgeons may release (cut) a tendon on the underside of the toe to prevent or treat sores on the sole of the foot.

**Rigid deformities.** Treatment may involve lengthening or cutting a tendon combined with shortening the bone or fusing the joint (arthrodesis), often followed by placement of a pin for 4 to 6 weeks to hold the toe in position while it heals.

## Recovery
After surgery, stiffness, swelling, and redness typically last 4 to 6 weeks. Weight-bearing is initially limited to the heel for short distances, and longer walking is restricted during this time. Elevating the foot promotes healing and reduces discomfort. A fused joint loses its ability to bend, and the toe may appear slightly longer or shorter than before.
