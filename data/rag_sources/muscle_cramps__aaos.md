# Muscle Cramps

## Overview
A muscle cramp is an involuntary contraction of a muscle that occurs suddenly and does not relax. These involuntary spasms can affect any skeletal muscle under voluntary control, involving part or all of a muscle, or several muscles in a group.

## Most Commonly Affected Areas
- Back of the lower leg/calf (gastrocnemius)
- Back of the thigh (hamstrings)
- Front of the thigh (quadriceps)
- Feet, hands, arms, abdomen, and along the rib cage

## Cause
Although the exact cause remains unknown, researchers believe inadequate stretching and muscle fatigue lead to abnormalities in the mechanisms that control muscle contraction.

### Contributing Factors
- Poor conditioning
- Exercising or working in intense heat
- Dehydration
- Depletion of salt and minerals (electrolytes)

### Inadequate Stretching and Muscle Fatigue
When poorly conditioned, muscle fatigue can alter spinal neural reflex activity. Overexertion depletes oxygen supply, causing waste buildup and spasm.

### Heat, Dehydration, and Electrolyte Depletion
Sweat drains the body's fluids, salt, and minerals (potassium, magnesium, calcium), potentially causing muscle spasms.

### Risk Factors
- Infants, young children, and people over age 65
- Being ill or overweight
- Overexerting during work or exercise
- Certain medications (pseudoephedrine, diuretics, statins)
- Endurance athletes and older people performing strenuous activities

## Symptoms
- Range from slight twitching to agonizing pain
- Cramping muscles may feel hard and appear visibly distorted
- Duration: a few seconds to 15 minutes or longer; may recur multiple times

## Home Remedies
- Stop the triggering activity
- Gently stretch and massage the cramping muscle
- Apply heat to tense muscles or cold to sore/tender muscles
- Hydrate and replenish electrolytes through sports drinks, milk, or foods like yogurt, bananas, lentils, and spinach

## Prevention

### Warm Up
Always warm up before stretching. Examples include slowly running in place or walking briskly for a few minutes.

### Calf Muscle Stretch
- Lean forward against a wall with one leg in front of the other
- Straighten your back leg and press your heel into the floor; front knee is bent
- Hold for 15 to 30 seconds
- Keep both heels flat on the floor; point back foot toes toward front foot heel

### Hamstring Muscle Stretch
- Sit up tall with both legs extended straight in front
- Place palms on floor and slide hands toward ankles
- Hold for 30 seconds
- Keep chest open and back long; reach from hips
- Do not round your back or lock your knees

### Quadriceps Muscle Stretch
- Hold onto a wall or chair for balance
- Lift one foot and bring heel toward buttocks
- Grasp ankle and pull heel closer to body
- Hold for 30 seconds
- Keep knees close together; never stretch to the point of pain
- Do not arch or twist your back

## When To See Your Doctor
Although most muscle cramps are harmless, they can indicate serious conditions including:
- Spinal nerve irritation or compression
- Hardening of the arteries
- Narrowing of the spinal canal
- Thyroid disease
- Chronic infection
- Cirrhosis of the liver
- Lou Gehrig's disease (rare)

Seek medical attention if cramps are severe, happen frequently, respond poorly to simple treatments, or are unrelated to obvious causes like strenuous exercise or dehydration.
