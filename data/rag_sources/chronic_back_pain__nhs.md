# Back pain

Back pain, particularly lower back pain, is very common. It usually improves within a few weeks, but for some people it can persist or recur.

## Causes

Back pain typically stems from injuries such as muscle strains. Medical conditions such as slipped discs, sciatica, or ankylosing spondylitis may also trigger it. Rarely, serious issues like fractures, cancer, or infections are responsible.

## When to seek medical help

**See a GP if:**
- Pain doesn't improve after a few weeks of home treatment
- Pain interferes with daily activities
- The pain is causing you concern
- Pain is accompanied by unexplained weight loss, a lump or change in shape, symptoms that get worse at night, or pain located in the upper back

**Contact NHS 111 or a GP urgently if:**
- You have sudden, severe pain, or symptoms such as fever and chills, which warrant prompt attention

**Call 999 or go to A&E if you have back pain along with:**
- Numbness or tingling around (or loss of feeling in) both legs
- Numbness or tingling around (or loss of feeling in) your genitals or bottom (anal area)
- Loss of bladder or bowel control (incontinence, or difficulty peeing)
- Chest pain
- The pain was caused by a serious accident, such as a car crash

## Self-help strategies

**Do:**
- Stay active and try to continue with your daily activities
- Use anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen
- Apply ice packs or heat packs to ease pain
- Try gentle stretches
- Try activities such as walking, swimming, yoga or pilates

**Don't:**
- Avoid prolonged bed rest

## Professional treatments

If self-help measures don't work, a GP may suggest:
- Physiotherapy
- Manual therapy
- Group exercise programmes
- Psychological support such as cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
- Procedures to reduce pain from a nerve
- Surgery, if there's an underlying condition that could benefit from it
