# Bursitis

## Definition & Overview
Bursitis is when the fluid-filled sacs (bursa) that cushion the joints have become painful and swollen (inflamed). The condition typically resolves within weeks with home treatment.

## Symptoms
The affected joint may display several indicators: dull, achy pain; tenderness; warmth relative to surrounding skin; swelling; and increased discomfort during movement or pressure. Redness may also appear. Most commonly affects shoulders, hips, elbows, or knees.

## Self-Care Treatment
Three main strategies help manage bursitis: resting the joint while avoiding pressure-inducing activities; applying ice wrapped in cloth for approximately 10 minutes, repeated several times daily; and taking pain relievers like paracetamol or ibuprofen. Extra cushioning around the area during sleep provides additional support.

## When to Consult a Doctor
Seek GP evaluation if symptoms persist or worsen after 1-2 weeks of self-treatment, when fever or chills develop, if joint movement becomes impossible, or when experiencing severe, sharp, or shooting pain.

## Medical Assessments & Treatments
Healthcare providers examine the joint and may extract fluid samples via needle aspiration to check for infection or conditions like gout. Treatment options include antibiotics (if infection-related), steroid injections, fluid removal, or in rare severe cases, surgical drainage or removal of the bursa.

## Prevention Strategies
Maintain a healthy weight, clean joint wounds, properly warm up before exercise, use padding for pressure activities, and take regular breaks from repetitive movements.
