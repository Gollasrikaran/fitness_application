# NICE NG59: Low back pain and sciatica in over 16s — assessment and management

NICE does not publish a guideline specifically titled "degenerative disc disease." Degenerative disc disease is one of the most common underlying causes of chronic, non-specific low back pain (with or without sciatica), and is managed under the general NICE NG59 guideline on low back pain and sciatica. The full guideline is published by NICE at nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59; this content reflects that guidance as reproduced on the NCBI Bookshelf.

## Self-Management

Healthcare professionals should provide people with advice and information, tailored to their needs and capabilities, to help them self-manage their low back pain, at all steps of the treatment pathway. This includes information about the nature of the pain (which, for degenerative disc disease, is often related to normal age-related changes rather than serious pathology) and encouragement to continue with normal activities.

## Risk Stratification

Consider risk stratification tools (such as the STarT Back tool) at first contact for each new episode of low back pain, to inform shared decision-making about stratified management — simpler support for people likely to improve quickly, and more complex, intensive support (such as exercise programmes) for those at higher risk of a poor outcome.

## Imaging

Do not routinely offer imaging (such as MRI showing disc degeneration) in a non-specialist setting for people with low back pain, since imaging findings of disc degeneration are common in people without symptoms and do not reliably guide management. Imaging should only be considered in specialist settings if the result is likely to change management.

## Exercise

Consider a group exercise programme (biomechanical, aerobic, mind-body, or a combination of approaches) for people with a specific episode or flare-up of low back pain, taking their specific needs, preferences and capabilities into account. Exercise and continued activity is generally recommended over rest for pain arising from degenerative disc changes.

## Manual and Psychological Therapy

Manual therapy and cognitive behavioural psychological therapy should only be considered as part of a treatment package that also includes exercise.

## Pharmacological Management

- Consider oral NSAIDs at the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible period.
- Do not offer paracetamol alone.
- Do not offer opioids for managing chronic low back pain.

## Invasive Treatments

- Do not offer spinal injections for managing low back pain.
- Do not offer spinal fusion for people with low back pain outside of a randomised controlled trial context, including for pain attributed to disc degeneration alone.
