# Tennis elbow

Tennis elbow is a condition that causes pain around the outside of the elbow. It usually goes away with rest but can sometimes last over a year. This NHS page covers tennis elbow specifically; it does not also cover golfer's elbow.

## Symptoms

- pain on the outer part of the elbow, ranging from mild to severe
- tenderness or swelling in the elbow
- pain that spreads into the forearm
- difficulty fully straightening the arm
- symptoms worsen with lifting, gripping, or twisting the wrist

Tennis elbow is more common in people aged 35 to 54.

## Causes

Tennis elbow is caused by inflamed tendons in the elbow, usually from repetitive gripping and wrist-twisting movements - for example from typing, manual work, racquet sports, or playing a musical instrument.

## Self-care

- avoid the activities that make the pain worse
- take paracetamol, or use an anti-inflammatory gel, for pain relief
- apply a hot or cold pack to the elbow for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
- try simple exercises such as gently bending the arm
- wear a forearm strap or brace

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you still have elbow pain after resting your elbow and trying self-care treatments for at least 2 weeks.

## Treatment

- a GP may prescribe anti-inflammatory tablets
- physiotherapy - usually offered after 6 weeks without improvement - may include massage, stretching exercises, and ultrasound therapy
- surgery may be considered if symptoms persist for 6 to 12 months despite other treatment
