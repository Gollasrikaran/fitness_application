# Sciatica

Sciatica happens when the sciatic nerve, which runs from your lower back to your feet, is irritated or compressed. It typically improves within a few weeks or months but may recur.

## Symptoms

Sciatica usually affects the bottom and back of one leg. Symptoms can include:
- sharp, burning pain running down the leg
- tingling
- numbness
- weakness

The pain may be worse when moving, sneezing or coughing, and symptoms may develop suddenly or gradually.

## Causes

- A slipped disc (the most common cause)
- Spinal stenosis
- Spondylolisthesis
- Back injury

## Self-care

**Do:**
- continue with normal activities as much as possible
- try gentle exercises for sciatica
- start doing gentle exercise as soon as you can
- put a hot or cold pack on the painful areas
- ask a pharmacist about painkillers

**Don't:**
- do not sit or lie down for long periods

## When to see a GP

See a GP if the pain has not improved after treating it at home for a few weeks, is getting worse, or is stopping you doing normal activities.

## When to get urgent help

Go to A&E or call 999 if you have sciatica symptoms in both legs, severe weakness or numbness in both legs, numbness around or under your genitals or bottom, or you lose control of your bladder or bowels.

## Treatment

- Exercises and stretches, often guided by a physiotherapist (free on the NHS)
- Psychological support if needed
- More advanced options for persistent cases: radiofrequency denervation, epidural injections, or decompression surgery

## Prevention

Stay active, use proper technique when lifting, maintain good posture, and avoid smoking to help reduce the risk of sciatica.
