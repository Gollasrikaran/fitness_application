# Adult Spondylolisthesis of the Low Back

## Overview
Spondylolisthesis occurs when one of the bones in your spine — called a vertebra — slips forward and out of place. This condition most commonly affects the lower back and may produce no symptoms, or cause pain ranging from mild to severe.

## Anatomy
The spine consists of stacked vertebrae connected by flexible intervertebral disks that function as shock absorbers during movement.

## Description
When vertebrae slip forward, the spine becomes unstable, potentially causing pain and accelerating bone spur formation and arthritis.

## Causes

### Degenerative Spondylolisthesis (DS)
Age-related wear weakens intervertebral disks and supporting ligaments. Women over 50 are more susceptible, with higher incidence among African-American populations.

### Isthmic Spondylolisthesis
A stress fracture in the pars interarticularis — typically occurring during adolescence — causes vertebral slippage. About 4% to 6% of the U.S. population has spondylolysis and spondylolisthesis.

## Symptoms

**Degenerative**: Leg weakness when standing or walking, numbness, tingling, and pain relieved by forward bending or sitting.

**Isthmic**: Low back pain considered activity-related, sometimes accompanied by leg pain.

## Diagnosis
Doctors use medical history, physical examination, X-rays, MRI scans, and CT imaging to confirm diagnosis.

## Treatment Options
Nonsurgical approaches include physical therapy, medication, and steroid injections. Surgery becomes an option after 3-6 months of failed conservative treatment, with success rates exceeding 85%.
