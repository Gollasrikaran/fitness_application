# Osteoarthritis

## What It Is
Osteoarthritis is the most common type of arthritis in the UK and involves joints becoming painful and stiff due to breakdown of protective cartilage.

## Main Symptoms
Joint pain and stiffness are primary indicators, along with potential swelling, tenderness, and a grating or crackling sound when moving the affected joints. The condition frequently affects knees, hips, and hand joints.

## Risk Factors
Several elements increase susceptibility, including joint injuries, advancing age, family history, obesity (which strains weight-bearing joints like hips and knees), female sex, and prior joint damage from conditions like rheumatoid arthritis.

## Diagnosis Approach
GPs typically assess symptoms and examine joints. Suspicion increases if patients are 45+ with worsening pain during joint use and morning stiffness lasting under 30 minutes. Additional imaging or blood tests may rule out alternative conditions.

## Treatment Options
The condition cannot be cured but may stabilize or improve gradually. Management ranges from lifestyle modifications — regular exercise, weight loss, appropriate footwear — to physiotherapy and pain medication. Severe cases may require surgical intervention.

## Prevention Strategies
While complete prevention is impossible, risk reduction involves maintaining activity (150 minutes weekly of moderate aerobic exercise plus strength training), proper posture, avoiding prolonged static positions, and managing weight.
