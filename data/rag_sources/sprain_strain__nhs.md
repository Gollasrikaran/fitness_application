# Sprains and Strains

## Overview
Sprains and strains are common injuries that affect the muscles and ligaments. Most can be treated at home without needing to see a GP.

## Symptoms
It's likely to be a sprain or strain if you have pain, tenderness or weakness - usually around your ankle, foot, wrist, thumb, knee, leg or back - along with swelling, bruising, being unable to put weight on the injury or use it normally, and muscle spasms or cramping.

## Sprain vs Strain
A sprain is a stretched or torn ligament. Sprains are most common in your wrists, ankles, thumbs and knees.
A strain is an overstretched or torn muscle (sometimes called a "pulled" muscle). Strains most often happen in your feet, legs or back.

## Self-Treatment: PRICE Therapy
For the first 2 to 3 days, follow the five steps known as PRICE:
1. **Protection** - protect the area from further injury, for example by using a support
2. **Rest** - stop exercising and try to avoid putting weight on the injury
3. **Ice** - apply an ice pack (or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a towel) to the injury for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
4. **Compression** - wrap a bandage around the injury to support it
5. **Elevation** - keep it raised on a pillow as much as possible

During this time, try to avoid heat (such as hot baths), alcohol, running, or massage, as this may increase swelling. When you can move the injured area without pain stopping you, try to keep moving it so the joint or muscle does not become stiff.

## Pharmacist Advice
A pharmacist can give advice about medicines that can help with pain, such as paracetamol, or medicines that can help with pain and swelling, such as ibuprofen (as tablets or a gel you put on the skin).

## Recovery
After 2 weeks, most sprains and strains will feel better. Try not to do any strenuous exercise, such as running, for up to 8 weeks, as there's a risk of further injury. Severe sprains and strains can take months to heal fully.

## When to Get Further Help
Call NHS 111 or get help from 111 online if:
- the pain or swelling gets worse, or does not start to improve after a few days of treating it at home
- you cannot put any weight at all on the injury or you cannot use the affected joint
- the area feels stiff and it's hard to move it
- you have a very painful lump on the muscle
- you have tingling or loss of sensation
- the injury has not improved after 2 weeks

See a GP if your symptoms have not started to improve within a few days, or you're not sure if it's a sprain, strain or something more serious.

Call 999 or go to A&E if:
- you heard a snap, grinding or popping noise at the time of injury
- the joint or muscle has changed shape or you cannot use it at all
- the area is numb, appears a different colour, or you cannot move your fingers or toes
- you think you have broken a bone
