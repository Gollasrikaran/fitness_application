# Neck Pain

## Description
The neck has significant mobility and supports the weight of the head. Because it has less protection than other regions of the spine, it remains vulnerable to injury and to disorders that cause pain and restricted motion. For many people, neck pain is temporary and resolves naturally; others require medical diagnosis and treatment for symptom relief.

## Anatomy
The spine consists of 24 stacked bones called vertebrae. Seven small cervical vertebrae begin at the base of the skull and form the neck. Vertebrae, ligaments, and muscles provide spinal stability and enable movement. The spinal cord extends from the base of the skull to the lower back through the spinal canal, and nerve roots branch from the spinal cord through openings in the vertebrae (foramina), transmitting messages between the brain and muscles. Flexible intervertebral disks between the vertebrae act as shock absorbers during activities like walking or running.

## Cause
Neck pain can stem from soft-tissue issues (muscles, ligaments, nerves) or from skeletal and disk problems. Common causes include:

- **Soft-tissue injuries**: Strains or sprains affecting muscles and ligaments.
- **Prolonged wear and tear**: Cervical spondylosis (arthritis of the neck), which can potentially lead to cervical spondylotic myelopathy (spinal cord compression) or cervical radiculopathy (a pinched neck nerve).

Rarely, infections or tumors cause neck pain, and neck problems can sometimes generate pain in the upper back, shoulder, or arm.

### Cervical Disk Degeneration (Spondylosis / Arthritis)
Cervical disks act as shock absorbers between the neck bones. Degeneration typically occurs in people age 40 and older — as spinal disks age they lose height and begin to bulge, and their water content decreases, causing drying and weakening, which reduces cushioning capacity. When the outer rim of a disk weakens, it may bulge outward and press on the spinal cord or nerve roots — a herniated cervical disk — which can cause arm and leg pain, numbness, tingling, and weakness. Cervical spondylosis is extremely common: more than 85% of people over the age of 60 are affected.

### Injury
The neck's flexibility and its role in supporting the head make it extremely vulnerable to injury from motor vehicle accidents, diving mishaps, contact sports, and falls. Rear-end automobile collisions may produce hyperextension or hyperflexion of the neck beyond normal limits — commonly called whiplash. Regular seatbelt use helps prevent or minimize neck injury. Common neck injuries include sprains and strains of muscles and ligaments, pinched nerves (cervical radiculopathy), and — in severe cases — fractures or dislocations that can damage the spinal cord and cause paralysis.

## When to Seek Medical Care
After severe neck pain from an injury (motor vehicle accident, diving accident, or fall), a trained professional such as a paramedic should immobilize the patient to prevent further injury and reduce the risk of paralysis — immediate medical care is essential, and time is most critical following trauma.

Seek immediate medical care when an injury causes radiating neck pain down the arms and legs. Seek evaluation from a medical professional for radiating pain or numbness in the arms or legs causing weakness, even without significant neck pain — subtle signs of a serious spine problem include difficulty with fine motor skills such as changes in handwriting, trouble fastening buttons, and dropping keys or cups.

Regardless of cause, seek medical care when neck pain is:
- Continuous and persistent (not resolving over time)
- Severe
- Accompanied by radiating pain or numbness down the arms or legs
- Accompanied by headaches, numbness, tingling, or weakness
