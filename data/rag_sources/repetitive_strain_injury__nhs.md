# Repetitive strain injury (RSI)

Repetitive strain injury (RSI) is a term used for pain caused by repeated movement of part of the body. It generally gets better on its own, though recovery can be helped along with appropriate self-care. RSI can affect the shoulders, elbows, forearms, wrists, hands, and fingers, and symptoms usually develop gradually.

## Symptoms

- pain, which may feel like burning, aching or throbbing
- stiffness
- weakness
- tingling, pins-and-needles, or numbness
- cramp
- swelling

## Causes

- repetitive activities, such as hairdressing, decorating, typing, or working on an assembly line
- sports involving repetitive movements, such as golf or tennis
- poor posture, for example while working
- regular use of hand-held power tools

Repetitive or strenuous work does not always cause RSI.

## Self-care

**Do:**
- try to keep active and gradually increase how much activity you do
- take paracetamol or an anti-inflammatory painkiller
- put a cold pack on the affected area for up to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours
- talk to your employer about making changes to your workspace or work pattern

**Don't:**
- avoid resting completely for more than a few days, as this can weaken the affected area

## When to see a GP

See a GP if you have symptoms of RSI and they are not going away or are getting worse.

## Treatment

- **Physiotherapy** - exercises, massage, and posture advice; free physiotherapy is available through the NHS
- **Occupational therapy** - to identify underlying causes and suggest changes to your workspace
- Steroid injections, or in rare cases surgery, for more severe or persistent cases
